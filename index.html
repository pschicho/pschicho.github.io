Hi, I’m Philipp

I’m a theoretical physicist at the University of Geneva. I develop precision methods in thermal field theory to predict gravitational waves from phase transitions in the early universe, so that a future measurement by LISA can test physics beyond the Standard Model.

Since 2024, I have led the TPC group, funded by my SNSF Ambizione project PRECLISA. I am also a core member of the LISA Cosmology Working Group.

      Thermal field theory, Particles, and Cosmic phases group

      My research group investigates quantum theories under extreme conditions, exploring the dense and hot regimes that appear in compact star astrophysics and the early universe, while also seeking signs of new physics at the electroweak scale. Studying theories at finite temperature and density requires combining perturbative and non-perturbative methods to unravel their phase structure. Developing and applying this interplay of theoretical tools is at the heart of the Thermal field theory, Particles, and Cosmic phases group.

      Precision thermodynamics for cosmological phase transitions
      Understanding the connection between the matter content of the universe and its thermal history is a major challenge in cosmology. The detection of gravitational waves (GW) from binary black hole mergers at LIGO has invigorated efforts to scope the universe in a broad temperature range and answer the question: is there new physics at the electroweak scale? The electroweak phase transition (EWPT) in beyond the Standard Model (BSM) theories could be strongly first order, possibly via complex multi-step transitions, and thereby explain baryogenesis and in turn the baryon asymmetry of the universe.
      Such a transition would also source a stochastic GW background that propagates freely to us from the early universe (cf. top figure). Its signal is expected in the millihertz band of the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA), a space-based detector of three spacecraft trailing the Earth around the Sun (cf. animation) that ESA plans to launch in 2035. Turning such a measurement into a statement about particle physics requires precise predictions of the phase transition thermodynamics.

      DRalgo: automated 3d EFT framework for thermal field theories
      DRalgo is an algorithmic implementation that constructs an effective high-temperature theory for generic models. The Mathematica package automates the matching of 4d thermal field theories to their 3d EFTs at next-to-leading order and, since its latest update, of higher-dimensional operators at one loop.

      How fast does the WallGo? A package for computing wall velocities in first-order phase transitions
      WallGo is an open source code for the computation of the bubble wall velocity and bubble wall width in first-order cosmological phase transitions. The main WallGo Python package determines the wall velocity and width by solving the scalar field(s) equation of motion, the Boltzmann equations and energy-momentum conservation for the fluid velocity and temperature.
